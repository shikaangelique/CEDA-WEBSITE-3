# Project in Spotlight: Strengthening Mineral Revenue Governance in Ghana's Mining Districts

## Our Ongoing Partnership with the Africa Centre for Energy Policy (ACEP)

CEDA's work with ACEP represents a sustained, multi-phase intervention to ensure that mineral wealth in Ghana translates into real, inclusive development for the communities that bear the brunt of mining activity. What began as a coalition-led diagnostic and capacity-building project across six mining districts has evolved into a forward-looking, pre-revenue intervention in Tano North — a brand-new mining district hosting one of West Africa's largest gold deposits.

## Phase 1 — The EICCG Project: Improving the Efficiency of Minerals Development Fund (MDF) Disbursement and Utilisation

### *Empowering and Inspiring Citizens for Community Governance*

**Lead Organisation:** Centre for Extractives and Development, Africa (CEDA)
**Coalition Partners:** Ghana Anti-Corruption Coalition (GACC), African Disability Institute (ADI), Northern Patriots in Research and Advocacy (NORPRA)
**Funded By:** Africa Centre for Energy Policy (ACEP)
**Status:** Completed — Final Project Report submitted

### The Problem We Set Out to Solve

Ghana's mining districts host the country's most lucrative resource extraction, yet many of them remain underdeveloped. The Minerals Development Fund (MDF), established under Act 912 (2016), channels 20% of mineral royalties toward mining-affected communities through MMDAs, the Office of the Administrator of Stool Lands (OASL), and Mining Community Development Schemes. In practice, however, MMDAs lacked the technical capacity to forecast MDF inflows, disbursements were irregular and bureaucratic, citizen participation was minimal, and corruption risks mirrored those plaguing the District Assemblies Common Fund. The result: mineral wealth was failing to deliver broad-based, equitable development.

### What the Coalition Did

The project combined diagnostic research, technical capacity-building, citizen oversight, and inclusion advocacy across **six mining districts**:

- Sefwi Wiawso Municipal Assembly *(Western North)*
- Tarkwa-Nsuaem Municipal Assembly *(Western)*
- Prestea Huni-Valley Municipal Assembly *(Western)*
- Amansie West District Assembly *(Ashanti)*
- Birim North District Assembly *(Eastern)*
- Asutifi North District Assembly *(Ahafo)*

### Headline Achievements

**A new forecasting model for mining districts.** CEDA developed a **seven-year moving average MDF forecasting model** in Excel, using historical production data from the Minerals Commission and validated royalty records from OASL. For the first time, district Coordinating Directors, Planning Officers, and Budget Officers had a practical tool to project future royalty inflows, integrate them into District Medium-Term Development Plans (DMTDPs), and end the era of off-budget, ad-hoc spending.

**Local Accountability Networks (LANets) established in all six districts.** Led by GACC, this initiative trained traditional leaders, women's groups, youth associations, persons with disabilities, faith-based groups, and community volunteers in social audits, community scorecards, expenditure tracking, and project verification. LANets are now institutionalised platforms for ongoing citizen monitoring of MDF-funded projects, and have already flagged stalled construction, poor signage, and quality concerns — prompting corrective action by Assemblies.

> *[INSERT PHOTO: LANet training session / community monitoring activity]*

**18 MDF-funded projects field-verified.** Across the six districts, the team inspected three projects each — covering education, health, water and sanitation, roads, and economic infrastructure — to evaluate real socio-economic outcomes against MDF investments.

**A GEDSI-first approach to inclusive planning.** ADI led training for district planning units, works and engineering departments, and social welfare officers on Gender Equality, Disability, and Social Inclusion (GEDSI) — applying Ghana's Accessibility Standards for the Built Environment and the Persons with Disability Act, 2006 (Act 715). Engineers received hands-on guidance on universal design: ramps, handrails, signage, accessible WASH, and gender-responsive budgeting principles. Asutifi North has since begun incorporating disability-friendly design into health infrastructure planning.

**Peer learning between mature and emerging mining districts.** The project convened officials from prospecting Upper East districts (Talensi, Nabdam, Bongo, Bolgatanga Municipal) with experienced districts (Asutifi North, Tarkwa-Nsuaem) — building readiness in districts that anticipate MDF inflows but currently lack mining revenue.

### Stories from the Field

A handful of MDF investments showed exactly what the Fund can do when used strategically:

- **Sefwi Wiawso — Mpomam Road Stabilisation.** Commercial drivers' weekly earnings rose from approximately GHS 300 to GHS 800 — a **167% increase** — after years of vehicle repairs caused by deplorable road conditions. The road now serves around 1,000 vehicles daily, improving market access for farmers reaching Takoradi, Kumasi, and Accra.

- **Sefwi Wiawso — Bosomoiso KG Block.** A dilapidated, leaking, insect-infested early childhood facility serving 136 children was replaced with a modern, weather-resilient, child-friendly block. Enrolment rose to **158 children**, and parents who once paid up to GHS 660/term for private kindergartens now access free, local early-childhood education.

- **Sefwi Wiawso — Borehole Rehabilitation.** Six dysfunctional boreholes serving roughly 1,000 people were restored at the height of the COVID-19 pandemic — replacing dependence on a contaminated stream and enabling new income pathways for women through commercial water bagging.

- **Prestea Huni-Valley — Government Hospital Vertical Expansion.** A one-storey medical block was transformed into a two-storey facility with new ENT, wellness, and herbal medicine services. Annual patient attendance grew from **46,000 in 2023 to 50,000 in 2024**, serving a catchment of over 40 communities and ~40,000 residents.

- **Prestea Huni-Valley — Bogoso Information & Communication Centre.** The Centre served **10,000 students in 2022, 12,000 in 2023, and 16,000 in 2024**, with curricula spanning HTML coding, robotics, AutoCAD, 3D printing, virtual reality, cybersecurity, and digital marketing — and reaches over 1,300 educators annually through professional development.

- **Tarkwa-Nsuaem — Essuoso 6-Unit Classroom Block.** Commissioned in October 2021, the block resolved a severe overcrowding crisis (60+ students per room, double-shift teaching during COVID-19). Enrolment grew from **857 in 2020 to 1,084 learners**.

### What the Coalition Found — and What Needs to Change

The Corruption Risk Assessment, the field verification of 18 MDF-funded projects, and stakeholder consultations surfaced a set of systemic findings now informing CEDA's policy advocacy:

- **62% of stakeholders had never received any information** on MDF inflows, disbursements, or funded projects — pointing to a systemic transparency failure.
- **Disbursement bottlenecks** persist despite Act 978 requirements for monthly royalty payments.
- **Fragmented spending** across many small projects diluted impact and prevented "legacy" investments.
- **Political interference** distorted project selection and procurement.
- **Most MDF-funded projects were not labelled or branded** as MDF investments — undermining citizen oversight.
- **Many projects lacked ancillary facilities** — disability ramps, water, sanitation, electricity — limiting inclusive use.
- **Misalignment between Act 912, Act 481, and Act 936** creates overlapping mandates and ambiguity in disclosure and enforcement.

The full report sets out **12 policy recommendations**, including streamlined disbursement timelines, transparency dashboards published quarterly, mandatory MDF project signage, mainstreaming MDF allocations into DMTDPs, institutionalising accessibility audits, and harmonising the legal frameworks governing mineral revenue.

## Phase 2 — Strengthening Extractives Governance for Sustainable Mineral Development in Tano North Municipal Assembly (TNMA)

**Lead Organisation:** Centre for Extractives and Development, Africa (CEDA)
**Funded By:** Africa Centre for Energy Policy (ACEP)
**Project Timeline:** **March 2026 – December 2026** (10 months)
**Proposal Submitted:** 9th February 2026
**Status:** New phase — building directly on lessons from the EICCG project

> *[INSERT PHOTO: Tano North landscape / Ahafo North Gold Mine site / community engagement]*

### Why Tano North, Why Now

The Tano North Municipal Assembly is a **brand-new mining district**, set to host Newmont's Ahafo North Gold Mine — recognised as the **best unmined gold deposit in West Africa** prior to its development. This is a rare opportunity to intervene **before mineral revenues begin flowing**, rather than after the governance challenges have set in.

The lessons from Obuasi, Kenyasi, and the six EICCG districts are clear: mineral discovery does not automatically translate into improved development outcomes. Without strong governance systems, proactive forecasting, inclusive planning, and active citizen oversight, mineral wealth can entrench inequality, fuel community–company conflict, and produce a localised resource curse.

### What the Project Will Do

The intervention is built on five sequential phases:

1. **Project Inception** — kick-off, M&E framework, risk management.
2. **Community Entry & Training Needs Assessment** — formal entry with TNMA, the Duayaw Nkwanta and Yamfo Traditional Councils, Newmont Ghana, opinion leaders, women's groups, youth, and persons with disabilities. A structured Training Needs Assessment will diagnose capacity gaps in mineral revenue governance.
3. **Document Review & Analysis** — deep review of the Minerals and Mining Act (Act 703), the Compensation and Resettlement Regulations (L.I. 2175), the MDF Act (Act 912), the OASL Act (Act 481), the Local Governance Act (Act 936), the Ahafo North ESIA, and TNMA's own development plans and procedures.
4. **Community Sensitisation & Capacity Building** — targeted training for TNMA officials on mineral revenue forecasting, planning, budgeting, and reporting (using the seven-year moving average model developed under Phase 1); resource governance training for community-based CSOs, traditional leaders, and opinion leaders; and townhall-style sensitisation on Free, Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC), compensation, and resettlement processes.
5. **Project Closure** — practical tools, guidelines, learning materials, and a policy-relevant report for TNMA and national stakeholders.

### Targeted Outcomes

- **14 TNMA staff trained** on mineral revenue forecasting, planning, budgeting, utilisation, and reporting.
- **16 local accountability actors trained** on resource governance, social accountability tools, and citizen oversight.
- **2 community sensitisation events** on FPIC, compensation, and resettlement.
- **100 community members** directly engaged through sensitisation activities.
- **430 user-friendly learning materials** distributed to host communities.
- A district equipped with a **mineral royalty forecasting tool, spending guidelines, and best-practice frameworks** before mineral revenues begin to flow.

### Why This Matters

Phase 2 transforms Phase 1 from a diagnostic-and-corrective intervention into a **preventive model**. The seven-year moving average forecasting tool, the LANet citizen oversight model, the GEDSI integration framework, and the policy lessons from six mature mining districts are now being deployed proactively in a district that has not yet received its first MDF inflow. If this works in Tano North, it becomes a replicable template for every future mining district in Ghana.

## The Continuing Partnership

CEDA's collaboration with ACEP is built around a single conviction: that resource governance must be **anticipatory, inclusive, and locally accountable**. The EICCG project showed what happens when mining districts lack the capacity, transparency, and citizen oversight to manage mineral revenues well. The Tano North project is now putting those lessons to work before the problem arises.

Together, these two phases — diagnostic and preventive, retrospective and forward-looking — form the foundation of a long-term programme to ensure Ghana's mineral wealth delivers durable, equitable development for the communities that host it.
